# Design Quality Standards

## Quality floor

Every design must be truthful, task-oriented, accessible, responsive, and implementable. Visual originality never compensates for a broken task, fabricated claim, missing state, or inaccessible interaction.

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the default web target. Automated checks are necessary but insufficient; review keyboard flow, focus visibility, screen-reader announcements, zoom/text resizing, touch targets, reduced motion, and error recovery.

## Positive design requirements

Every approved direction must identify:

- the primary audience and highest-value tasks;
- the experience’s specificity thesis;
- the design signature and why it belongs to this subject;
- the content hierarchy and realistic states;
- the evidence behind important visual choices;
- the constraints and accessibility target;
- the exceptions to suspicious defaults.
- which decisions are verified, assumed, or still unknown;
- the falsifier for every high-risk assumption controlling a primary task or interaction model.

Evidence references must resolve to exact registered records. A source hash or artifact digest proves which bytes were reviewed, not that the source supports the decision or that users will succeed.

## Distinctiveness standard

Distinctiveness comes from meaningful choices in typography, composition, density, geometry, imagery, language, motion, and subject-specific artifacts. A direction is not distinct merely because it has a different color palette.

Patterns are allowed when they are justified. The system flags unexplained repetition and default convergence; it does not ban cards, gradients, serif type, rounded corners, or dark themes universally.

## Review principle

Use separate findings for requirements, UX, accessibility, content, specificity, visual craft, system consistency, responsiveness, and implementation feasibility. Never collapse them into a single average score.
