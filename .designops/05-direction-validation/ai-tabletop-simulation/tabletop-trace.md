# AI tabletop trace — hypothesis generator, not human evidence

## Setup

The role-play holds the shared content fixture, answer labels, score semantics, required states, and access paths constant. It compares only the frozen Room Beacon and Private Relay interaction models.

Each trace uses five fictional roles:

- a session starter who wants to begin quickly;
- a confident screen-facing participant who answers early;
- a quieter screen-facing participant who initially disagrees;
- a blind forehead holder or private active player;
- an access-path participant who uses tap-only or a screen reader in the appropriate role.

No trace is a claim that people will behave this way. It is a structured way to expose the product conditions that require real observation.

## Trace A — sober, ordinary party distraction

| Pressure point | Room Beacon hypothesis | Private Relay hypothesis | Must test with people |
|---|---|---|---|
| First round | Shared reading can create a fast common focus once “group reads; holder commits” is understood. | Explicit active-player ownership is more familiar but may take longer to restore a shared focus. | Unaided start, role recall, time and assistance to first valid answer. |
| Dissent | An early confident reader can become the de facto narrator; the quieter member may not affect the holder’s commit. | Private commitment can protect independent judgment but may make the rest of the group passive. | Who speaks before commit, whether dissent is acknowledged, and whether the commit authority is understood. |
| Recovery | Blind tilt/tap creates a meaningful duplicate-commit risk; feedback must be useful to holder and group without leaking the card. | Direct tap reduces ambiguous movement but shifts risk to premature handoff and exposed prior result. | One-commit reliability, fallback comprehension, protected handoff. |
| Rematch | Shared spectacle may create stronger collective momentum if it remains legible. | Orderly turns may improve fairness but need a shared result payoff to avoid feeling like solo trivia. | Rematch choice and stated reason, never an AI-predicted rate. |

## Trace B — alcohol-affected, high noise/distraction

| Pressure point | Room Beacon hypothesis | Private Relay hypothesis | Must test with people |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention | A large quote and animated group can overshadow a perimeter-only game marker or completed truth language. | A role strip can carry context but may be dismissed as generic app chrome if not understood. | Capture-context explanation across screenshot, recording still, photograph, and reasonable crop. |
| Inhibition | Grip changes, exaggerated tilt, or a rush to commit can increase accidental/duplicate answers; tap fallback must be immediate and equivalent. | Tap is more predictable under noise but can still be premature; a privacy shutter can be skipped if transition state is unclear. | Misfire, duplicate, and recovery observations; no alcohol-effect rate may be inferred. |
| Noise and memory | Spoken consensus, role cues, and full attribution are likely to compete with ambient speech and divided attention. | Private reading protects the active player’s comprehension but can reduce group participation and invite coaching or peeking. | First-read and state explanation under frozen noise/lighting/distance conditions. |
| Social pressure | The holder may follow the loudest voice rather than a collective decision. | Public score and turn order can create a different pressure pattern even when judgment is private. | Dissent, ownership, and fairness observations before and after result. |

## Cross-cutting simulated findings

1. **Room Beacon has the higher upside and higher fragility.** Its shared-stage ritual directly tests forehead social value, but it concentrates risk in capture context, social dominance, sensor recovery, and access parity.
2. **Private Relay is the stronger countermodel, not the default winner.** It creates a more predictable control and handoff model but may lose the product-specific shared ritual and rematch energy.
3. **The difference is conditional, not categorical.** Noise, distance, content length, access path, role makeup, and individual alcohol response can change the balance. AI cannot supply those measurements.
4. **The research design must observe human behavior, not reward chaotic behavior.** Alcohol, speed, loudness, and peer pressure are stressors. They are never success metrics.

## Human-psychology gate result

| Gate | Simulation status | What remains required |
|---|---|---|
| Salient context and truth | Needs human test | Individual truth/context explanations and capture inspection. |
| One intended commit | Needs human test | Real accidental/duplicate-commit and recovery evidence. |
| Dissent before consensus | Needs human test | Facilitator observation of participation and authority. |
| Noise and memory budget | Needs human test | Frozen environment and correct-first-read evidence. |
| Role privacy and access parity | Needs human test | Role-specific accessible-participant completion and spoiler checks. |
| Repair without shame | Needs human test | Plain-language recovery and safe-next-action observations. |

The simulation does **not** select a direction, update the Direction gate, or authorize application work.
