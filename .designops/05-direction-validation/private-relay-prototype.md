# Private Relay — frozen low-fidelity countermodel

**Candidate ID:** `private-relay`
**Role:** countermodel
**Purpose:** Test whether a tap-first, role-explicit relay provides comparable social confidence and stronger accessible recovery without the selected direction's forehead-stage complexity.

This is a behavior-and-content prototype specification, not production UI or an approved visual system. Every content item, role script, state, access path, device condition, and capture condition is bound to `shared-content.json`.

## Interaction model

1. **Role rehearsal.** The session starter sees the current actor, private-reader, and next-player responsibilities. The active player can perceive the statement and owns the player or team score; each handoff protects prior content and outcome.
2. **Readiness.** Tap is primary. Extended-timer, untimed, reduced-motion, no-audio, and no-haptic choices are set before the round and do not alter score or truth semantics. Forehead tilt remains an optional equivalent format.
3. **Private active round.** A persistent role strip names the actor and game context. The private prompt, attribution, timer, and labeled verdict controls are visible only to the active player.
4. **Commit and recovery.** Labeled taps record one intended answer. Sensor loss, backgrounding, and orientation change preserve state; the privacy shutter protects previous content before recovery or handoff.
5. **Result and review.** A calm review sheet begins with the truth classification in words, then explanation, permitted source status, report, and a retained-session next action.
6. **Handoff and rematch.** A protected shutter precedes every next-player reveal, interruption recovery, and rematch handoff. No player may see a prior prompt or result while receiving their turn.

## Frozen scenarios

### PR-01 — unaided first answer

Start a pass-and-play session with the fixture's median-length playable card. The active player and group must identify role, privacy, and commit mechanics after the prepared prompt, without moderator clarification.

### PR-02 — control recovery and protected handoff

Complete a private answer, introduce sensor failure and background-resume conditions, and then hand off to the next player. The next player must continue without a duplicate answer, lost round, or disclosure of prior content/outcome.

### PR-03 — truth, capture, and operational-state comprehension

Show the same completed review states and capture set as Room Beacon. The participant explains card status, game context, and available report/recovery actions; then walks disputed, source-unavailable, removed, queued-report, offline, and corrupt-deck states.

### PR-04 — equivalent access

Run the fixture's tap-only, no-motion, extended-timer/untimed, reduced-motion, no-audio, no-haptic, VoiceOver screen-facing contributor, VoiceOver private pass-and-play, and TalkBack private pass-and-play paths. Preserve the same role-appropriate completion, score, truth review, report return, and privacy semantics as Room Beacon.

## Non-negotiable observations

- The protected shutter prevents previous-card and previous-result disclosure during handoff, interruption, and recovery.
- A reasonable pre-reveal capture retains game context but does not look like an authentic public statement or reveal authenticity.
- Accessibility settings and non-motion paths do not change answer, score, or truth-review semantics.
- Fabricated-for-game, authentic, disputed, source-unavailable, and removed states are explained in words and never by color alone.

## Comparison constraint

Use the same fixture content, timer, answer labels, score semantics, scenario order, moderator prompt, and post-task questions as Room Beacon. Only the interaction model and structural expression may differ.
